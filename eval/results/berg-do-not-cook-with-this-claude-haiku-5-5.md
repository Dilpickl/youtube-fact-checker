## [1:00] SUPPORTED: Industrial seed oil processing uses hexane, a solvent that is a component of gasoline.

> adding hexane which is a solvent that's in gasoline

Hexane (a petroleum-derived solvent) is widely used to extract oils such as soybean, cottonseed, and canola from seeds, and it is a minor component of gasoline, so the claim is accurate. The video's wording is loose, but the factual core, that hexane is used in seed oil processing and is a gasoline component, holds up.

- [Hexane - an overview (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/hexane): Describes hexane as a minor component of gasoline and states it is used as a solvent in extracting soybean, cottonseed, flaxseed, and other oil seeds.

## [4:03] CONTRADICTED: The oxidative stress from smoking persists for about 6 months to a year after quitting.

> the oxidative stress that occurs with smoking kind of poisons us for about 6 months to a year after quitting

A 2025 systematic review of 10 studies (2,750 participants) found oxidative stress markers improve within one to two weeks of quitting, faster than the 6-month-to-1-year window the video gives. Some airway and DNA damage can persist longer, but no source found supports the claim that oxidative stress keeps 'poisoning' the body for 6 months to a year after quitting.

- [Physiologic Recovery After Smoking Cessation: A Systematic Review of Inflammatory and Cardiovascular Biomarker Changes](https://www.cureus.com/articles/500877-physiologic-recovery-after-smoking-cessation-a-systematic-review-of-inflammatory-and-cardiovascular-biomarker-changes): Oxidative stress markers improved within one to two weeks of abstinence, while inflammatory markers declined over about two to 12 weeks and lipid and hematologic parameters over three to eight months.
- [Physiologic Recovery After Smoking Cessation: A Systematic Review of Inflammatory and Cardiovascular Biomarker Changes - PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13456119/): Oxidative stress markers, particularly F2-isoprostanes, were among the earliest to improve after cessation, with a significant decrease in 8-epi-PGF2α after 1-2 weeks.
- [Persistence of oxidant and protease burden in the airways after smoking cessation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2697135/): Levels of several oxidative stress markers in induced sputum were very similar in never smokers and healthy ex-smokers who quit more than 20 years earlier, suggesting oxidative stress declines over time after quitting.
- [Reduction in oxidatively generated DNA damage following smoking cessation - PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3104490): Smoking cessation reduced specific oxidative DNA damage markers by roughly 30-50 percent within about 11 weeks, in a small study of 19 participants.

## [4:34] CONTRADICTED: Seed oils damage mitochondria and cause insulin resistance, leading to fatty liver, obesity, heart disease and increased cancer risk.

> this is why seed oils lead to insulin resistance

The mechanistic idea that oxidized linoleic acid impairs mitochondria and insulin signaling comes mostly from cell and animal studies, and human evidence doesn't show seed oils causing insulin resistance, obesity, heart disease or cancer. Large meta-analyses of cohort and trial data instead link higher linoleic acid intake to lower coronary heart disease and type 2 diabetes risk, though one reanalysis of the Sydney Diet Heart Study trial found possible harm from substituting linoleic acid for saturated fat.

- [Dietary Linoleic Acid and Risk of Coronary Heart Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies (Circulation)](https://ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/circulationaha.114.010236): Concludes that dietary linoleic acid intake is inversely associated with coronary heart disease risk in a dose-response manner.
- [Use of dietary linoleic acid for secondary prevention of coronary heart disease and death: Sydney Diet Heart Study reanalysis and updated meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4688426): Reports non-significant trends toward increased coronary and cardiovascular death when linoleic acid replaced saturated fat, and finds no evidence of cardiovascular benefit in trials, a point that cuts against the claim's opposite direction only partly.
- [Seed Oils on Trial: Is the Panic Justified? (Sigma Nutrition)](https://sigmanutrition.com/seedoils/): Says feeding-trial meta-analyses found higher PUFA intake improved glycaemia and insulin resistance, and that the seed-oil-harm claims are not supported by the literature.
- [Seed Oils and Blood Sugar: What the Controversy Is Really About](https://www.healthspectra.com/seed-oils-and-blood-sugar-what-the-controversy-is-really-about/): Cites a Diabetes Care meta-analysis of 20 cohort studies finding higher linoleic acid levels linked to lower type 2 diabetes risk, and notes theoretical oxidized-LA mechanisms lack direct human evidence.
- [Linoleic Acid: A Narrative Review of the Effects of Increased Intake in the Standard American Diet and Associations with Chronic Disease](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10386285/): Advocates the mitochondrial and oxidized-LA hypothesis, citing mostly animal and mechanistic studies, which supports the claim's mechanism only as a hypothesis.

## [5:05] MISLEADING: Seed oils are not burned as fuel in the body but are stored and accumulate in it.

> the seed oils are not being burned up as fuel in the body so they kind of get lodged and stored

Linoleic acid from seed oils is indeed stored in fat tissue, and adipose levels reflect intake, but it is also oxidized for energy (one review says roughly 60% goes through beta-oxidation), so the claim that it is not burned as fuel is false. The video's framing would leave viewers thinking seed oils are never used by the body, which the sources do not support.

- [Increase in Adipose Tissue Linoleic Acid of US Adults in the Last Half Century (Advances in Nutrition)](https://advances.nutrition.org/article/S2161-8313(23)00114-X/pdf): Adipose tissue linoleic acid concentration reflects dietary intake and is strongly correlated with per-capita LA intake, supporting that LA accumulates in fat stores.
- [Whole body oxidation of dietary fatty acids: implications for energy utilization (American Journal of Clinical Nutrition)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002916523350172): Dietary linoleate is measured as being oxidized in the whole body, and linoleate and alpha-linolenate are described as more rapidly beta-oxidized than other fatty acids.
- [Linoleic acid: structure, synthesis, metabolites, function, foods](https://www.tuscany-diet.net/lipids/fatty-acids/linoleic-acid/): Linoleic acid is abundant in adipose tissue where its concentration reflects dietary intake, and almost 60 percent of it is oxidized by the beta-oxidation pathway.
- [Linoleic Acid in Food and Nutraceuticals](https://periodical.knowde.com/linoleic-acid-in-food-and-nutraceuticals/): Linoleic acid, like other fats, can be stored in adipose tissue as triglycerides and later mobilized and used for energy, such as during fasting or exercise.

## [3:32] SUPPORTED: Fatty acids from seed oils get incorporated into and replace components of cell membranes.

> the seed oils can actually get into the membranes and replace the membranes

Dietary linoleic acid from seed oils is well documented to be incorporated into cell membrane phospholipids, where it replaces other fatty acids, and membrane composition tracks dietary intake. The video's literal wording, that seed oils 'replace the membranes,' overstates this, since the evidence shows partial substitution of membrane fatty acids, not wholesale replacement.

- [Dietary linoleic acid and human health: Focus on cardiovascular and cardiometabolic effects - ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021915019315758): Review states that within cells, linoleic acid can be incorporated into cell membrane lipids, and in cell membranes it is incorporated at the sn-2 position of phospholipids.
- [Incorporation profiles of conjugated linoleic acid isomers in cell membranes and their positional distribution in phospholipids - ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1388198110002076): Study notes that dietary-type polyunsaturated fatty acids are incorporated into membranes and that the incorporated fatty acids replaced 18:1 in most cases.
- [Dietary linoleic acid modulates liver plasma membrane unsaturated fatty acid composition, phosphatidylcholine and cholesterol content, as well as glucagon stimulated adenylate cyclase activity - ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0271531785801433): Increasing dietary linoleic acid increased linoleic acid content of liver plasma membrane phosphatidylcholine in the study.
- [Linoleic Acid - an overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/pharmacology-toxicology-and-pharmaceutical-science/linoleic-acid): States that the type of fatty acids predominating in a cell membrane is influenced by the fatty acid composition of the diet.
